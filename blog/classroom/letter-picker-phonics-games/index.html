Letter Picker: 8 Phonics Games

Turn letter practice into a game with a random letter picker. 8 zero-prep phonics games for kindergarten and first grade.

It’s 8:15 in the morning. Twenty-two kindergarteners are sitting on the rug, and you’ve got twelve minutes of letter practice before the wiggling wins. The flashcards come out, and you can feel the energy drain in about forty seconds. They’ve seen these cards a hundred times. You have too.

Here’s what changed that morning block: a Letter Picker wheel on the big screen. Same letters, same sounds, completely different energy. The spin adds a tiny jolt of suspense, and suddenly letter practice feels like a game instead of a drill. Below are eight games that take zero prep. Load your letters, project the wheel, and play.

1. Mystery letter sound-off

Spin one letter. Kids don’t say its name, they say its sound. First round, the whole class choruses it. Second round, spin three letters and let them blend the sounds into a word if they can. Two minutes, done, and it’s the fastest warm-up I know. Cover the wheel after the spin so the answer isn’t sitting on the screen. The kids who peek learn nothing, and everyone knows it.

2. Beginning-sound scavenger hunt

Spin a letter, then give kids sixty seconds to point at something in the room that starts with it. S for scissors, T for table, P for the pencil sharpener. The first week they’ll shout the sound and point at nothing. That’s fine. The hunting is what cements the letter-sound connection, not getting it right on the first try. Use the Random Name Picker to choose who spins, and the loudest kid doesn’t get to run every round.

3. Rhyme family spin

Swap the wheel entries for word families: -at, -in, -og, -ed. Spin one, and the class generates as many real words as they can in a minute while you write them on the board. Kids who struggle with blending get a scaffold because the ending stays the same. Strong readers get to show off a little. Everyone’s working the same wheel, so nobody’s singled out.

4. Air-write the letter

Spin a letter and the whole class “writes” it in the air with big arm movements, calling out the strokes: “down, around, across.” Then they trace it on a partner’s back with a finger. Sounds silly, works great. Letter formation sticks better when the whole body is in on it, and the spin picks the letter so nobody can claim you only chose the hard ones.

5. Letter bingo without the caller fatigue

Call the letters by spinning instead of drawing tiles from a bag. Project the wheel so kids watch each letter land, which doubles as a mini reading exercise. Kids cover the letter on their bingo cards. First full row wins. The usual bingo chaos stays, but the “did you already call that one?” arguments stop, because the spin history is right there on screen.

6. Blend and digraph builder

Once single letters feel easy, load the wheel with blends and digraphs: sh, ch, th, wh, ph, st. Spin one, and kids name a word that starts with it. Ship, chair, thumb. This is the bridge to real reading, and it’s where a lot of kids stall. Keeping it a spin game keeps the stakes low. A wrong answer gets a gentle redirect, no buzzer, no scoreboard.

7. Silly sentence sprint

Spin three letters. Kids build one sentence using as many words as they can that start with those sounds. “Silly Sam sat silently” counts even if the grammar wobbles. Read the best ones aloud and let the class vote for the funniest by cheering. This one works for first graders who have outgrown single-letter games, and it sneaks vocabulary work in under the cover of comedy. When the class needs to reset between rounds, the Brain Break Wheel buys you five minutes of movement before the next spin.

8. Exit ticket spin

Line up at the door. Spin one letter per kid, and each one names a word that starts with it before stepping out. Thirty seconds per kid, tops. The line moves, nobody pushes, and you get a daily read on who’s solid and who’s shaky. I’ll be honest: the first day, half of them will freeze. By Friday they’re racing each other to answer. Run it with the Student Picker in no-repeat mode and every kid gets a turn across the week.

Try game one tomorrow

Load your current letter set into the Letter Picker tonight and run the mystery letter sound-off first thing tomorrow. It takes two minutes. Watch which letters the kids hesitate on. That hesitation list is your small-group plan for the rest of the week, handed to you by a wheel.
