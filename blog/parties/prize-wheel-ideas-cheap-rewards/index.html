Prize Wheel Ideas: 30 Cheap Rewards

30 prize wheel rewards that cost nothing or next to nothing — classroom freebies, family coupons, party prizes, and team rewards people actually want.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published October 12, 2026 Updated October 12, 2026

On this page

  1. Classroom rewards that cost you zero dollars
  2. Family prizes your kids will fight over
  3. Party prizes that aren’t plastic junk
  4. Team prizes when there’s no budget
  5. Experience prizes beat things
  6. Cheap tangible prizes that don’t feel cheap
  7. Setting up the spin so it feels fair

Last spring I ran a birthday party game and did what everyone does: I spent forty bucks at the party store on a bag of plastic trinkets. Whistles, sticky hands, little dinosaurs with one leg bent wrong. The kids grabbed them, dropped them on the lawn, and forgot them before the cake came out.

The prize that started an actual argument over who won? A paper coupon that said “you pick dinner this Friday.” Cost me nothing. That was the whole lesson. Prizes don’t need a budget. They need to feel like they were made for the winner.

Load these into a Prize Wheel and spin instead of handing things out by hand. The wheel does the deciding, which means nobody accuses you of playing favorites, and the suspense of the spin makes even a silly coupon feel like winning something real.

Classroom rewards that cost you zero dollars

Teachers run more prize wheels than anyone I know, and most of them are buying prizes out of their own pockets. Stop doing that. The rewards below cost nothing and get a bigger reaction than any treasure-box toy.

  1. Ten extra minutes of recess. This is the gold standard. Kids would trade a toy every time.
  2. Line leader for the day. For second graders this is basically royalty.
  3. Choose the read-aloud book. One kid picks, the whole class hears it.
  4. Sit-with-a-friend pass. Good for one period, and yes, they’ll use it immediately.
  5. Homework pass. One assignment, gone. The gasp this one gets never gets old.
  6. Class DJ. Picks the cleanup music for a week.

A note from teachers who do this a lot: weight the wheel. Put the big ones (recess, homework pass) on one or two thin segments and the small ones wider. If homework passes land every day, they stop being special. You can adjust segment sizes on the Prize Wheel page before you spin.

Family prizes your kids will fight over

Family game night needs stakes. Without a prize, somebody checks out by round three and starts asking for a tablet. These are all free, and in my house they land harder than anything from a store.

  1. Stay-up-late pass. Thirty extra minutes on a school night. The 9-year-old would rob a bank for this.
  2. You pick dinner. Winner chooses Friday’s meal, no vetoes. (We learned this rule the hard way.)
  3. Remote control night. Total control of the TV for one evening. No negotiating.
  4. Skip-a-chore coupon. One chore, your choice, deferred to a parent. Redeem anytime.
  5. Solo outing with a parent. Winner picks the destination: park, ice cream, bookstore. This one consistently beats the tangible stuff, which tells you something.

I’ll be honest: number 9 caused a genuine feud between my two kids over whether “no negotiating” included pausing. It did not. Set the terms when the coupon is won, not when it’s used.

Party prizes that aren’t plastic junk

If you’re running party games for a group of kids, the prize table is where the mood lives or dies. Here’s the thing: kids don’t want stuff. They want status and power for about ten minutes.

  1. Lucky-dip grab bag. Fill it with dollar-store items, but the dip itself is the prize. Reaching into a bag blind beats being handed something every time.
  2. Temporary tattoos. A sheet costs almost nothing and kids treat them like real ink.
  3. Extra game turn. Winner gets a bonus round in the next game. Simple, and it keeps the energy up.
  4. Spin-again token. Lets the winner spin the prize wheel one more time and keep the better result. Kids love this more than any single prize on the list.
  5. Trade-up card. Winner can swap their prize with anyone else’s, no refusals. The drama this creates is half the fun.

When you’ve got more kids than prizes, use the Random Name Picker in no-repeat mode to decide spin order. Everyone gets a turn, nobody spins twice before everyone spins once, and the “it’s not fair” complaints drop off fast.

Team prizes when there’s no budget

Office and team wheels are a different animal. Nobody wants a plastic trophy on their desk. Adults want time and convenience, which luckily cost the company nothing if you’re the one running it.

  1. Leave-early pass. Ninety minutes, one Friday. This will be the most contested segment on your wheel.
  2. Best parking spot for a week. If your office has a lot, the front spot is weirdly powerful.
  3. Coffee run. The team buys your drink of choice for a week. Sounds small. Feels enormous.
  4. Lunch-break extension. An extra hour, once. People hoard this one like currency.
  5. Playlist control. Winner picks the office or shop music for a week. Somebody will abuse this. That’s the point.

One warning: check with whoever approves time off before you put 17 and 20 on the wheel. A prize you can’t honor is worse than no prize at all.

Experience prizes beat things

This is the pattern across every list above. A thing gets forgotten. An experience gets talked about. If you’re stuck, replace any tangible prize with one of these.

  1. Movie night, winner’s pick. They choose the film and the snacks. Full authority.
  2. Park picnic. You bring sandwiches, they pick the park.
  3. Bake-together session. Winner picks the recipe. You handle the mess together.
  4. Game night with winner’s rules. They choose the game and can change one rule. House rules get delightfully weird.

Number 25 has produced some of the funniest evenings I know. One family I know played a version of cards where every 7 meant everyone switched seats. Total chaos. Still talked about a year later.

Cheap tangible prizes that don’t feel cheap

Sometimes you do want something to hand over. Fine. Just keep it under a few dollars and make it specific.

  1. Book-swap pick. Winner gets first pick from a box of donated or thrifted books.
  2. Art-supply grab bag. A handful of good markers or a fresh sketchbook beats ten plastic toys.
  3. Printed award certificate. Sounds corny. Kids frame these. I’m serious.
  4. Sticker pack. Dollar store, one pack per winner. Let them choose from three designs.
  5. A seedling. A small potted herb or flower. Costs almost nothing, and the kid who wins it will water it like it’s their job.

Setting up the spin so it feels fair

A prize wheel only works if people trust it. Three things make that happen.

First, show the wheel before you spin. Let everyone see the segments and read the prizes. Surprises are fun, but secret prizes make people suspicious.

Second, use the spin history. When someone claims “that already came up,” point at the history on screen. Arguments end immediately. It’s the same reason the Random Name Picker keeps a no-repeat mode. Visible history settles disputes.

Third, decide the rules before the first spin. Can you win twice? What happens if it lands on a prize that’s already gone? Announce it once, write it down, and don’t change it mid-game. The wheel is the referee. You’re just its assistant.

Load it up and spin

The prize wheel that worked best in my experience had exactly twelve segments, a mix of big and small, and a group of kids who were way too invested in a homework pass. Total cost: one sheet of paper and ten minutes of typing.

Type your prizes into the Prize Wheel, show it to the room, and spin. Watch what happens when it slows down near the segment everyone wants. That gasp is the whole reason wheels beat envelopes.
