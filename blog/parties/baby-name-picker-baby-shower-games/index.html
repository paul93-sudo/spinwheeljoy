Baby Shower Games: 10 Ways to Use a Baby Name Picker

Baby shower name games make guests groan. A Baby Name Picker Wheel turns them into 10 quick party games people actually play, from vintage-name lotteries to sibling drafts.

It’s 2pm on a Saturday and the baby shower is already stalling. Someone is guessing belly size. Another table is arguing over diaper flavors. The guest of honor smiles politely and you can see it on her face: she did not want another diaper raffle. She wants something easy, not embarrassing, and over in ten minutes.

That’s where the Baby Name Picker helps. You load names, spin, and let the room do the rest. No speeches. No long workbooks. Just short games that give people a reason to look at each other instead of their phones. I’ll be honest, it works best when you keep the rounds tight and the stakes low. Here are ten ways to use it at the shower without anyone rolling their eyes.

1. The name lottery: guests bring one name

This is the entry ticket. Everyone writes a single baby name on a card when they arrive and drops it in a bowl. Load all of them into the wheel. Spin three times. Those three names go on the mom-to-be’s real shortlist.

Guests pay attention because their card could appear. It feels like a draft, not like homework. Sounds silly, works great.

2. The vintage revival: grandma’s generation

Ask one person to bring four names from their parents’ or grandparents’ era. Think Edith, Arthur, Mildred, Harold. Put them on the wheel with four modern picks the couple already likes. Spin.

The game: who remembers a relative with that name. You get stories, not just name lists. It takes five minutes and people leave with a name they didn’t expect to like.

3. The middle-name draft

First names carry all the pressure. Middle names don’t. Collect middle-name ideas from guests and spin one to pair with the family’s chosen first name.

My cousin spun “Mae” with “Lucia” and everyone agreed it clicked. If it lands on something odd, you laugh and move on. Low risk, high fun.

4. The sibling draft: let the kids play

Big siblings can wreck a shower if they are bored, or make it the best part if you give them a job. Let them submit three names each. The rule is loose: no video game characters, but otherwise anything goes. Spin.

The winner gets to “officially propose” one name during dinner. You get buy-in, and the older kid stops trying to rename the baby “Pikachu” at full volume.

5. The guilty-pleasure round

Everyone has a name they secretly like but won’t admit. Anonymous slips, no names on the paper. Shuffle them into the wheel. Spin twice.

The room guesses why someone might have suggested it. You will hear names that never make it onto a formal list, and sometimes one sticks. Here’s the thing: the game works because it’s anonymous.

6. The veto vote: family feud, settled

Two sides of the family disagree. Each side loads five names into the wheel. Spin one from each side, head-to-head. Winner moves on.

If you need a tiebreaker at the end, run a quick round on the Decision Wheel. The point is not to let one loud uncle pick everything. The wheel spreads the choices enough that everyone stays.

7. The theme round

Pick a theme. Nature. Cities. Old Hollywood. Candy. Load six or eight names that fit and spin.

This keeps the room from spiraling into fifty random ideas. When the theme is done, switch. You get contrast, not chaos.

8. The gender-reveal double spin

If the shower is also the reveal, put boy names on one list and girl names on another. Spin once each.

You announce one name from each set. If you don’t know which baby you’re having, you plan for both. Either way you leave with names attached to a moment people remember.

9. The nickname test

Spin a full name. Pause. The room shouts the nickname it would produce. If the nickname is a disaster, veto and spin again.

This saves you from “Theodore” becoming “Theo” in a family that already has three Theos, or “Harriet” turning into “Harry” and confusing the dog. Practical and quick.

10. The final three: spin to break the tie

By the end of the shower, most couples have three names they really like and can’t choose between. Load those three. Spin.

I’ll be honest: the wheel is not deciding your baby’s name. You are. But when you spin, watch your reaction. If you wince when it lands, you have your answer. If you laugh with relief, that’s the one. It’s an old trick and it still works.

Keep the rounds short. Four minutes per game, two spins max, then move on. If you want another way to settle small choices during the party, try the Yes or No Wheel for food, music, or who opens the next gift.

Open the Baby Name Picker, load your lists, and let the room play. You’ll spend less time managing the shower and more time watching people actually participate.
