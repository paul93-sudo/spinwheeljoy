Galentine's Day Wheel: Plan the Girls' Night

Every February our group chat turns into a 200-message argument about dinner, and nobody books anything. A wheel settles the host, dinner, activity, gift swap, and timing in six spins, before the group chat eats another weekend.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published October 27, 2026 Updated October 27, 2026

  6. Spin for the timing
  7. Spin for the one photo everyone keeps
  8. Try the Galentine’s day wheel

Last February our group chat hit 200 messages and we still had no plan. Maya wanted tacos. Priya wanted pasta. Ana said she was fine with either, then voted against both. Someone suggested a movie, someone else said movies were boring, and by the time anyone mentioned a date, two people were busy and one was already annoyed. We did not have a Galentine’s night that year. We had a group chat about a Galentine’s night, which is not the same thing at all.

Here’s the thing. A girls’ night fails for the same reason a couples’ date fails: too many small decisions stacked on top of each other, and nobody wants to be the one who picks wrong. Host, dinner, activity, gift, timing. Each one is easy on its own. Together they turn into a week of texting and a night that never happens. Put them on the Date Idea Wheel and spin them in order, and the whole night gets planned in about fifteen minutes. The wheel takes the blame for every pick, which is exactly what a group of friends needs. Nobody picked tacos over pasta. The wheel did.

Spin for who hosts

Host goes first because everything else follows from it. A host with a small apartment means a small guest list and takeout, not a sit-down dinner for eight. A host with a big kitchen means cooking together is on the table, literally. So spin for the host before you spin for anything else. Put every name on the wheel, including the friend who always says she will host next time. Next time is now, if the wheel says so.

Use the Random Name Picker for this draw if you want it to feel clean and final, especially when the group is bigger than four. A wheel with eight names still works, but a name draw settles the host question in one spin with no debate about whose turn it is. At our house, the wheel gave Ana the host spot last year, and Ana was the friend who had been quietly hoping someone else would volunteer. She hosted, she cooked soup, and she told us later that being picked made it easier than volunteering would have. Volunteering feels like asking for work. Being picked feels like a job with a start and an end.

One rule that keeps this fair: the host does not also cook everything, buy everything, and clean everything. The host provides the place. The rest of the jobs get spun separately, below. A host who does it all will not host again next year, and then you are back to the group chat.

Spin for dinner first

Dinner goes second, right after the host, because the host’s kitchen decides what kind of dinner is even possible. Spin for the dinner type before anyone opens a menu or a recipe app. Put six options on the wheel: tacos, pasta, sushi, pizza, salad, and soup. Six keeps the spin honest. Twelve turns it back into the same argument you were trying to escape.

Our wheel landed on tacos last year, and tacos turned out to be the right pick for a group for a reason I had not thought about before I saw it happen. Tacos let everyone build her own plate, so nobody had to argue about spice, cheese, or meat. The friend who eats no meat made her own. The friend who puts hot sauce on everything made hers. Nobody compromised, because tacos do not ask anyone to compromise. Pasta asks everyone to agree on a sauce. Tacos ask everyone to agree on a table.

If the wheel lands on a dinner that truly does not work with the host’s kitchen, you get one re-spin. One. A taco night at a host with no stove still works, because tacos can be takeout. A sushi night at a host with no plates does not, and one re-spin fixes that without reopening the whole debate. Take the second result and move on. A night with a second-choice dinner still happens. A night with five re-spins never gets planned.

Spin for the activity after dinner

Once dinner is set, spin for what happens after it. Put six activities on the wheel: dance, movie, craft, game, spa, and walk. The wheel landed on dance for us, which fit a taco night in a way a quiet craft night would not have. Tacos are loud food. People are already standing up, already talking, already moving around the kitchen. A dance playlist kept that energy going. A movie would have stopped it cold, and then everyone would have sat in a row in the dark after an hour of talking. That is a fine night, just a quieter one than tacos started.

Here is where groups usually go wrong. They pick the activity first, fall in love with it, and then try to bend dinner around it. A spa night after a heavy pasta dinner feels sleepy. A walk after sushi in February feels cold. Dinner first, activity second, and the two fit together because the second decision respects the first one. It is a small order change, and it saves the night from feeling like two separate plans glued together.

If your group is bigger than six, spin for the activity in two rounds: one spin picks the type, and a second spin picks the game, the movie, or the playlist inside that type. Two spins still beat twenty texts. And if the activity needs supplies, like craft paper or a board game, the supplies go on the bring list in the next section, not in a new group chat thread.

Spin for the gift swap, with a cap

A Galentine’s gift swap is where a good night gets awkward. One friend brings a twenty-dollar candle. Another brings a two-dollar card. Both feel bad for different reasons, and nobody says so out loud. So before you spin for the gift type, agree on a cap out loud. Fifteen dollars, twenty dollars, whatever fits the group. Write it in the chat. Then spin.

Put six gift types on the wheel: candle, book, mug, soap, plant, and card. Our wheel landed on candle, which was a relief, because a candle at fifteen dollars is a good candle and a good night, while a plant at fifteen dollars is a plant that needs light the recipient may not have. The cap is what makes the spin fun instead of stressful. Without it, the wheel feels like a dare. With it, the wheel feels like a game.

I’ll be honest about one more thing. A gift the wheel picks beats a gift you stressed over for a week, most of the time. Not because the wheel is wise. Because a gift picked fast comes with no scorekeeping attached to it, and scorekeeping is what actually ruins gift swaps. Nobody remembers whether the candle smelled like vanilla or pine by March. Everybody remembers whether picking it felt like a contest.

Spin for who brings what

This is the spin groups skip, and it is the one that decides whether the night feels shared or feels assigned to one tired friend. Put the jobs on the wheel and spin for each one: who brings drinks, who brings dessert, who brings the playlist, who brings the game, who brings the plates if the host is short. Spin each job separately, and write the result down where everyone can see it. A list in the chat, pinned, not buried under memes.

At our house, the wheel gave Maya drinks and dessert, Priya the playlist, and Ana the game, since Ana was already hosting and the host rule from the first section meant she did not also get the food run. That split took about two minutes to settle, and it ended the part of girls’ night planning where one friend quietly buys everything and then feels resentful on the night itself. The jobs were visible. The jobs were split. Nobody had to guess who was supposed to bring what, because the wheel had already said so.

If one job lands on the same person three times in a row, trade one of them. That is not cheating. That is the difference between a fair draw and a lopsided night, and a lopsided night is the thing you are trying to avoid. The wheel sets the split. Common sense keeps it fair.

Spin for the timing

Galentine’s Day is February 13th, by tradition, but your night does not have to be. February 13th is a Friday in some years and a work night in most of them. The Saturday before it is easier for everyone with a job, a kid, or a commute, and nobody at the table will care that the night happened on the 7th instead of the 13th. What people care about is whether everyone could actually come.

Put six timing options on the wheel: Friday, Saturday, Sunday, February 13, February 14, and brunch. Our wheel landed on February 13 last year, and February 13 worked because we spun early, in January, and everyone blocked the night before their calendars filled up. If your wheel lands on February 13, book it the day you spin, not the week after. Spinning early and booking late is the same mistake as never spinning at all, just with extra steps.

If the wheel lands on brunch instead of a night out, take it seriously. Brunch is cheaper, calmer, and easier for friends with kids or early mornings, and a Galentine’s brunch with good food and no rush is a real girls’ night in daylight. The point of the spin is to pick a time everyone can make, not to defend a date on a calendar.

Spin for the one photo everyone keeps

The last spin is for the photo that makes the night yours instead of generic. Put six photo ideas on the wheel: a group selfie, a silly pose, a table shot, a matching accessory shot, a before-and-after of the food, and a candid during the game. One spin, one photo idea, done. Our wheel landed on matching accessory, so we all wore the same cheap pink scarves Ana found in a drawer, and the photo from that night is still the one that shows up in everyone’s camera roll in February.

This is the spin people roll their eyes at, and then remember longest. A night with dinner, dancing, and gifts is a good night. A night with dinner, dancing, gifts, and one photo everyone keeps is a night that gets talked about next February. The photo does not have to be staged. It has to be specific to your group, and the wheel is good at forcing that choice because it will not let you pick the safe group selfie twice in a row.

Sounds silly, works great. A planned photo also stops the end-of-night phone shuffle, where everyone stands around asking someone to take just one more. One spin, one photo, and then everyone goes back to talking, which is what the night was for in the first place.

Try the Galentine’s day wheel

That is the whole system. One spin for the host, one for dinner, one for the activity, one for the gift swap, one for who brings what, one for the timing, and one for the photo. Seven spins, fifteen minutes, and the night is planned before the group chat eats another weekend. No 200-message argument, no night that never happens, no friend quietly doing everything.

Try it with the Date Idea Wheel the next time February starts creeping up on your group. Spin the host first, then work down the list in order, and pin the job list in the chat the day you spin. If a side call comes up while you plan, like who goes first in the gift swap or which of two movies makes the cut, the Decision Wheel handles that one too. And if dinner itself turns into its own debate before you even get to the date wheel, the What to Eat Wheel settles that one first. The night will still be yours next February if the plan was picked fast and the photo was picked personal. The wheel is how you get both.
