Moving Day Order Wheel: What Gets Packed First

The truck arrives Saturday at 8 and the whole house still needs packing. A moving day order wheel picks what gets packed first, what waits until last, and who does which job.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published November 1, 2026 Updated November 1, 2026

It is Tuesday night. The truck is booked for Saturday at 8 in the morning, and Maya is standing in the middle of the living room holding a roll of tape and no plan at all. Dan has already packed two boxes. One holds a lamp, three cookbooks, and a pair of shoes. The other holds a lamp. He packed the lamp twice because nobody decided what goes in which box.

I’ll be honest. Most houses pack like this. Everyone starts in a different room, everyone packs the thing closest to their hand, and by Friday night the kitchen is still full, the bathroom is still full, and the boxes are labeled “stuff” in thick marker. The order matters more than the speed. Pack the wrong rooms first and you spend the whole week living out of boxes you needed open.

That is the whole job of a moving day order wheel. It picks the packing order before anyone tapes a box shut, so the rooms you barely use get packed first and the rooms you live in stay usable until the last day.

Load the Decision Wheel with the rooms in your house. Spin once. That room gets packed first. Spin again for the next room. Write the order on a sheet of paper and tape it to the fridge. Nobody re-decides the order at 9 at night when everyone is tired and snippy.

Start with the rooms you barely use

The spare room goes first. Then the garage, the attic shelf, the hall closet with the board games. These rooms hold things nobody will miss this week, and that is exactly why they go first. A box of board games taped shut on Tuesday changes nothing about Wednesday. A box of coffee mugs taped shut on Tuesday ruins Wednesday morning.

Maya and Dan started with the spare room on Tuesday night and had it done in an hour. Guest bedding, two boxes of books nobody had opened in a year, a lamp that belonged in the living room anyway. One hour, one room gone, and the house still worked fine the next morning.

Here’s the thing. Packing a room you barely use first gives you an early win, and an early win matters on a week like this. The house looks emptier. The stack of sealed boxes by the door grows. Everyone can see progress instead of just feeling tired. Start with the hardest room and the week starts with a fight. Start with the spare room and the week starts with a finished room.

One rule for this phase. If you open a box in the spare room and find something you use every day, it does not go in the box. It goes back in the house until its room comes up in the order. The spare-room box holds spare-room things. The moment daily things start landing in early boxes, the order falls apart.

Let the wheel pick the first box in each room

Inside a room, the same problem shows up smaller. Someone stands in the bedroom and asks where to start. The closet? The dresser? The bookshelf? Ten minutes later nobody has packed anything and everyone has an opinion.

Put the box types on the wheel instead of the rooms. Books, games, photos, crafts, shoes, DVDs. Spin once. Books land. The first box in the bedroom is books. Everyone who packs in that room starts with books until the books are done. Then spin again for the next box type.

This spin stops the worst packing habit, which is the half-packed box. When everyone picks their own starting point, the house ends up with six boxes that are each one-third full. One box type at a time means one box gets filled, taped, and labeled before the next one opens. A full box stacks. A third-full box collapses under the next one in the truck.

Label each box with the room and the box type, in big letters. “BEDROOM - BOOKS” beats “stuff” every time. The people unloading the truck on Saturday do not know your system. They know how to read a box. Write it big enough to read from three feet away, on two sides of the box.

Books, decor, and the stuff you never touch

Every house has a layer of things that never move until moving week forces the issue. The vase from an aunt. The framed photo from a hallway nobody looks at. The stack of magazines on the coffee table that everyone steps around.

Pack this layer early, right after the spare rooms. It is light, it is easy to box, and it clears surfaces fast. A living room without vases and framed photos looks half-packed in an afternoon, and that changes how the rest of the week feels. The house starts to look like a move in progress instead of a mess in progress.

Sounds silly, works great. Pack the decor before the useful things and the useful things get easier to reach. Shelves empty out. Counters clear off. The kitchen table, which spent a month holding mail and keys and a plant, becomes a packing station with room to tape boxes.

One warning here. Do not pack the decor and then buy new decor for the new place in the same week. The new place will have its own walls and its own light. Wait until you live there a week, then decide what the walls need. Boxes of old decor and bags of new decor in the same truck is how a move doubles in size for no reason.

Clothes by season, not by closet

Closets are where packing order usually dies. Someone opens the closet, sees everything at once, and packs the winter coats in October or the swimsuits in January. Then the weather turns on Thursday and the coat is in a sealed box in the garage.

Split the closet by season before the wheel ever spins. Off-season clothes get packed first, in boxes labeled with the season. Winter coats in a November move go in early boxes. T-shirts stay out until the last two days. In a summer move, flip it. Coats and sweaters go early, shorts and sandals stay out.

The Decision Wheel can settle the close calls. One shelf holds jackets that are neither winter nor summer. Spin for it. One drawer holds scarves and gloves and a rain jacket. Spin for it. The wheel does not care about the weather forecast. It just picks a day, and a picked day beats a debated day when the truck comes Saturday.

Keep one bag out of the boxes entirely. Two outfits, sleep clothes, and a jacket for moving day itself. That bag rides in the car, not the truck. Everyone who has ever dug through six boxes at midnight looking for pajamas learns this rule once and never forgets it.

The kitchen goes last, except for the coffee kit

The kitchen is the last room to pack, and everyone knows it, and everyone still packs it too early. Someone gets excited on Wednesday, boxes the coffee maker, and Thursday morning the whole house is grumpy before the day starts.

So pack the kitchen in two passes. The first pass takes the things nobody uses daily: the baking pans, the serving bowls, the spice rack extras, the third set of mixing bowls. The second pass, on Friday night, takes the daily things: plates, cups, pans, the coffee maker.

Before the second pass, pack a coffee kit. Coffee maker, two mugs, filters, coffee, sugar, a spoon. One small box, labeled COFFEE KIT in big letters. That box rides in the car with the clothes bag. Saturday morning, before the truck is even half loaded, someone can make coffee in the new place. A house with coffee at 7 in the morning loads a truck a lot better than a house without it.

The same trick works for the bathroom. Toothbrushes, soap, towels, and toilet paper go in one bag that rides in the car. Toilet paper first, before anything else in the new place. Ask anyone who has moved. They will tell you the same thing.

Pack the open first box last and label it loud

The open first box is the box you will want at 9 on Saturday night, when the truck is empty, the beds are half built, and everyone is hungry and tired. It gets packed last, because it holds the things you need first.

Put the real things in it. Bed sheets and pillows for each bed. Phone chargers. A basic tool kit with a screwdriver and a hammer. Snacks and water bottles. Toilet paper and hand soap. A change of clothes for Sunday morning. If it fits in one box, it goes in the box. If it needs two boxes, label both OPEN FIRST and number them.

Write OPEN FIRST on all four sides of the box, in the thickest marker in the house. Then tell the truck loaders which box it is, out loud, before they start loading. The box goes in the truck last so it comes out first. A box labeled on one side, loaded first, and buried under a sofa is not an open first box. It is a rumor of a box.

Maya’s open first box saved their Saturday night. Sheets on the bed by 10, chargers plugged in, snacks on the counter, and everyone asleep in a real bed instead of on a pile of coats. The rest of the boxes waited until Sunday. That is fine. Boxes can wait. Sleep cannot.

Spin for the moving-day jobs too

Packing order is only half the week. Saturday itself needs jobs, and jobs need names, or everyone ends up carrying boxes while nobody directs the truck and nobody cleans the old place.

Spin for the jobs on Friday night. Loader, director, cleaner, food runner. The director stands at the truck and tells each box which room it goes to in the new place. The cleaner stays at the old place with a broom and checks every room before the keys go back. The food runner buys lunch and brings water. Lunch matters. A crew that eats at noon works until 6. A crew that skips lunch quits at 3 and blames the stairs.

The Random Name Picker works well here if the crew is bigger than the house. Friends, family, anyone who showed up to help. Spin for each job so nobody feels picked on and nobody gets stuck with the same job all day. Swap jobs at lunch. The morning loader becomes the afternoon director. Fresh legs and fresh eyes in both roles.

One last spin for the keys. Someone holds the old keys until the old place is checked, room by room, closet by closet. The Yes or No Wheel can settle the final question of the day: one more load, or call it done and order dinner. Spin it. Whatever lands, the house eats dinner in the new place that night.

Try the moving day order wheel

The truck does not care how fast you pack. It cares whether the boxes are full, labeled, and in an order that lets you live until Friday and sleep on Saturday night. Spare rooms first, one box type at a time, clothes by season, kitchen last with a coffee kit in the car, and an open first box packed last and labeled loud.

Load the Decision Wheel with the rooms in your house. Spin for the order, tape it to the fridge, and pack in that order even when someone gets a better idea at 9 at night. The better idea can wait for the new place. The truck comes Saturday at 8, and the house that packs in order is the house that sleeps in a real bed on Saturday night.
