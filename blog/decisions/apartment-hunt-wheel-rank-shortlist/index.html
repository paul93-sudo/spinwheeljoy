Apartment Hunt Wheel: Rank the Shortlist Without the Spreadsheet

Four finalists, one spreadsheet, and a tie at the top. Use the Decision Wheel to break apartment ties, order Saturday viewings, pick lease terms, and gut-check the winner — without reopening the spreadsheet for the ninth time.

It’s Saturday, 8:40am. You’ve got five viewings booked across town, a spreadsheet with 11 columns, and two finalists tied at 87 points each. You’ve re-sorted that spreadsheet twice this week. It still says 87 and 87.

Here’s the thing: the spreadsheet did its job. It got you to two. It can’t pick the winner. That’s what the Decision Wheel is for.

A quick honest note before we start: a wheel doesn’t replace the homework. Tour the places, check the water pressure, ask about the laundry situation. The wheel steps in after that: for the ties, the tour order, and the moments where more thinking just loops you back to the same two answers.

Break the tie between finalists

You’ve scored them. Maple St has the kitchen, Elm Ave has the parking. They’re tied. Put just the finalists on the wheel (two or three names, nothing else) and spin.

If the wheel lands on Maple St and you feel a little disappointed, that’s your answer. You’re not looking for permission; you’re looking for your gut to finally speak up. The spin just gives it a microphone.

Order your Saturday viewing route

Five viewings, one day, crosstown traffic. Which do you see first? Put the addresses on the wheel and let the order fall out in spin order, wildcard across the bridge and all. Your patience for a 40-minute drive is highest at 9am, so put the farthest one first if it wins.

Lease terms when the price break is real

Landlords love offering $50 less a month if you sign 15 instead of 12. Put only the terms you’d actually sign on the wheel: 12, 15, 18 months. Don’t add 24 “just to see” if you know you’ll hate it.

Spin once. If you catch yourself hoping it lands on 12, you just saved yourself an evening of math.

The must-have tradeoff

No listing has both parking and in-unit laundry. That’s just how this goes. Make a two-segment wheel: PARKING and LAUNDRY. Spin it and live with the answer for 24 hours.

Sounds silly. Works great, because the tradeoff is fake anyway. You’ll survive either way, and the spin forces you to name which one you’d actually miss on a Tuesday in February.

Move-in date when the landlord gives you two

First of the month or the fifteenth? The first means double rent overlap; the fifteenth means a week on your brother’s couch. Both work, neither is great. Spin it and stop polling your friends. They’ve never moved into this apartment.

Settle the roommate split on the winner

You want Maple St. Your roommate wants Elm Ave. You’ve each sent four long paragraphs. Put the two finalists on a shared wheel, open it on a call, and spin on speaker.

Agree on the rule before you spin, not after you see the result: one spin decides. That’s the only part that matters.

The gut-check spin

This is the whole article in one move. When you’ve decided but keep second-guessing: put the winner on a wheel with one thin “BACK OUT” segment and five wide “SIGN IT” segments. Spin.

You already know the point. If the narrow BACK OUT sliver wins and you feel relieved, back out. If you feel annoyed, sign. The Yes or No Wheel does the same job for smaller calls.

Two wheels for after you’ve signed

Moving day has its own fights. The Chore Wheel splits the packing and the shelf-building so one person doesn’t end up doing everything. And on night one, eating dinner surrounded by boxes, you’ll want the food question answered fast too.

Open the Decision Wheel, add your two finalists, and spin once. If the result disappoints you, you’ve already picked. Sign the lease.
