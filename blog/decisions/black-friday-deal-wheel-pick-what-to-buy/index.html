Black Friday Deal Wheel: Pick What You Actually Buy

Last November my cart held nine things and my budget held room for three. A Black Friday deal wheel sorts the budget, the big buy, the store order, and the cuts before the sale starts.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published October 27, 2026 Updated October 27, 2026

  4. Spin for buy now or wait

  6. Spin for gifts or yourself first
  7. Spin for the leftover money
  8. Try the Black Friday deal wheel

Last November, by Wednesday night, my inbox had 63 promo emails in it. I counted. Half of them said the sale ended at midnight. The other half said the sale started at midnight. My cart held nine things: a TV for the living room, shoes for my son, a coat I had looked at three times, headphones, a toy my daughter asked for in July, and four things I could not remember adding.

I had room in the budget for three of them. Maybe four, if I skipped takeout for two weeks and nobody got sick and the car stayed quiet. That is not a plan. That is a wish with a cart attached to it.

I’ll be honest, the year before that I bought the coat first because it was on top of the page. Then I bought the shoes because my son asked that morning. Then the TV sold out while I was still deciding, and I spent December annoyed at a TV I did not buy and a coat I did not need that much. The order I shopped in decided what I got. I never decided the order.

So this year I set the rules before the sale starts, with the Decision Wheel. One spin per call. The calls happen in order, and the order stops changing once the countdown timers start flashing.

Spin for the budget cap

Before you look at a single price, spin for the cap. Put four numbers on the wheel: $100, $200, $300, and $500. Pick numbers that fit your real month, not the month you wish you had. Spin once. That number is the cap for the whole Black Friday run. Shipping counts toward it.

Our wheel landed on $200 last year. That sounds small next to a TV, and that was the point. A $200 cap forced a choice I had been dodging for a month: TV or everything else. I could not buy the TV and the shoes and the coat and pretend the math would sort itself out later. The cap said no before the sale page could say yes.

Write the cap on a sticky note and stick it to your laptop. Not in a notes app. On the laptop, where you will see it when the cart total creeps up. If the cap is $200 and the cart says $247, something drops. That is the next spin, not a reason to move the cap.

One rule keeps this honest. You do not re-spin the cap because you saw a TV you liked. A cap you re-spin is not a cap. It is a suggestion, and suggestions do not survive a sale page.

Spin for the one big buy

Every Black Friday list has one item that eats the budget. One TV, one laptop, one coat that costs as much as the other four things combined. Spin for that item first, before the small stuff fills the cart and leaves no room for it.

Load the wheel with the big candidates only. Ours had TV, laptop, shoes, coat, toy, and headphones. One spin. The TV won, and that changed the whole weekend. The shoes and the coat moved to the wait list in the section below, and the toy got its own smaller budget from the gift list. The TV was not the item I wanted most on Monday. It was the item that needed the most money, and money spent on small things first is money the big item never sees.

If the big buy costs more than the cap, that is useful news, and it is better news in October than on Friday night. You have three real moves: raise the cap on purpose, before the sale, with a number you can still pay in December; split the buy across two paychecks if the store lets you; or drop the big buy and let the wheel pick a smaller big buy from the same list. What you do not do is buy the big buy anyway and call the overage a surprise.

Spin for which store first

A sale is not one store. It is five tabs, three apps, and a line at the mall if you go in person. The order you check them in decides what sells out before you get there. So spin for the store order too.

Our wheel has four stops: online, mall, outlet, and local. Spin once, and that store gets checked first. Spin again for second, and so on until you have an order. Last year online won first, and the TV was still in stock at 7 a.m. because I checked online before I drove anywhere. The year before, I drove to the mall first, stood in a line for a coat, and came home to find the TV gone.

Set a time limit per store. Twenty minutes per store, then move to the next one on the list. A store order without a time limit turns into two hours on one site, and two hours on one site is how the other three stores sell out while you compare two nearly identical TVs.

If you shop with a partner or a friend, split the list. You take the first two stores on the wheel order. They take the last two. Text each other the cart totals as you go, so the cap stays visible to both of you.

Spin for buy now or wait

Not every Black Friday price is the lowest price. Some items drop again in December. Some go back up the Monday after and never come down. Some were cheaper in October and the “deal” is just the normal price with a red tag on it. So spin for the timing call, one item at a time.

The wheel has three stops for this: buy now, wait, and check the price history. Buy now is for the item that sells out every year, the toy your daughter asked for in July, the size and color that never lasts past Friday morning. Wait is for the item with ten in stock, three colors left, and no line behind it. Check the price history is for the item you suspect was cheaper before, and it means you look up last month’s price before you pay this month’s price.

I keep a short rule here. If I cannot find last month’s price in two minutes, I treat the item as a wait. A deal that needs an hour of research to prove is not a deal I need on Friday. It is a December purchase with a November label on it.

If the wheel says wait, write the item and the price on the same sticky note as the cap. Check it again on the Monday after. If the price went up, you lost nothing you had planned to spend. If it went down, you buy it then, and the wait paid for itself.

Spin for what drops from the cart

This is the spin most people skip, and it is the spin that saves the budget. When the cart total passes the cap, spin for the cut. Load the wheel with the cart items, or simpler, with four calls: drop, keep, wait, and swap.

Drop means the item leaves the cart now, no second look. Keep means it stays and something else drops instead. Wait means it moves to the December list. Swap means it gets replaced by a cheaper version of the same thing, same size, same job, lower price.

Last year the cart hit $263 against a $200 cap, and the wheel landed on drop for the headphones. I liked the headphones. I did not need the headphones in November, and my old pair still worked. The headphones went to the December list, and I bought them in January with birthday money. The TV stayed. The shoes stayed. The cap held.

Do this spin in the cart, not in your head. In your head, every item feels needed. In the cart, with the total on screen and the cap on the sticky note, the wheel makes the call you keep avoiding. One spin per item over the cap, until the total fits. Then stop. A cart that fits the cap is a finished cart.

Spin for gifts or yourself first

Black Friday mixes two budgets that should not mix: gifts for other people and things for you. When they share one cart, the gifts lose, because your coat is right there and your nephew’s toy can wait. Or you lose, because you buy five gifts and go home with nothing you needed.

So spin for the order. The wheel has two stops: gifts first, or yourself first. Whichever lands, that group gets bought first, up to its own share of the cap. Split the cap before this spin. If the cap is $200, say $120 for gifts and $80 for you, or whatever split fits your list. Write both numbers on the sticky note.

Gifts first means the toy and the shoes leave the store before the coat gets a look. Yourself first means the coat gets bought and the gifts get whatever is left, and you say that out loud before you shop so nobody is surprised later. Either order works. What does not work is no order, where the loudest item on the page wins and the gift list gets whatever is left at midnight.

If your gift list lives on a wheel already, the Random Name Picker can set the gift order too: one spin per person, buy in that order, stop when the gift share of the cap runs out. The rest of the list becomes the December list, and December is a fine month to finish a gift list.

Spin for the leftover money

If the cap holds and the cart fits, there will be money left over. Not much. Maybe $30, maybe $60. Money left over without a job becomes takeout and a streaming subscription you forgot to cancel. So spin for the leftover before the sale ends.

The wheel has four stops: save, treat, gift, and bills. Save means the money stays in the account and you write down what it was for, so January you remembers it. Treat means one small thing, one dinner out, one book, and then the rest goes to save. Gift means one extra gift for the person at the bottom of your list, the one you almost skipped. Bills means the December credit card bill, which is the least fun answer and sometimes the right one.

Last year our leftover wheel landed on save, and the $43 sat in the account until January, when it paid for the headphones from the December list. That is the whole trick. Leftover money with a job is money. Leftover money without a job is a cart you forgot you filled.

If the wheel says treat, keep the treat under half the leftover. A treat that eats the whole leftover is just a fifth item in the cart with a nicer name.

Try the Black Friday deal wheel

This week, before the promo emails pile up again, load the Decision Wheel with your cap numbers. Spin for the cap, spin for the big buy, spin for the store order, and write all three on a sticky note. When the sale starts, you will not be deciding. You will be checking a list, and the list will hold.

If the cart passes the cap, spin for the cut instead of moving the cap. The TV you planned for beats the coat you saw first, and the gifts you planned for beat both of them if gifts won the order spin. That is a boring way to shop a loud weekend, and it is the reason the budget survives it.
