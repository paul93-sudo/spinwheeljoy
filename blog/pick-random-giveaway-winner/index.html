How to Pick a Random Winner for Your Giveaway (Fair and Transparent)

Run a fair, transparent giveaway draw in six steps: collect entries, clean the list, spin a random name picker, and document the result. Free tool, no sign-up.

Nothing kills a giveaway faster than suspicion. "Was it rigged?" is the first comment you'll get if the draw isn't visibly fair. Here's a simple six-step process for picking a random winner that anyone can verify — using a free tool, with no sign-up.

What makes a draw feel rigged (even when it isn't)

Most accusations don't come from actual cheating — they come from opacity. A screenshot of a name with "congrats!" and no explanation of how it was chosen invites doubt. People trust what they can watch happen. That's why the entire method below is built around one principle: the draw should be observable. If a skeptical entrant can't reconstruct how you got from entries to winner, the process needs more transparency.

Step 1: Define the rules before you start

Write down three things and publish them where entrants can see:

  • Who can enter (age, location, one entry per person?)
  • The deadline (date and time zone)
  • How the winner is picked ("one random draw from all valid entries using a random name picker, drawn live on [date]")

Clear rules prevent 90% of disputes.

Step 2: Collect entries in one list

Export your entries into a single list — one name or handle per line. The method depends on your platform:

  • Instagram/TikTok comments: export or copy the commenter handles into a spreadsheet. Many creators use a comment-export tool, then paste the handles.
  • Email newsletter: export your subscriber list (or the segment that entered) as CSV and take the name column.
  • Entry form (Google Forms, Typeform): the responses spreadsheet already gives you one row per entrant.

Wherever they come from, consolidate them in one place. This is also where you remove duplicates and invalid entries (bots, double entries, people who didn't follow the rules).

Tip: Number your entries. "Entry #1 … Entry #214" makes the draw auditable — the winner is a number anyone can count.

Step 3: Use a real random picker

Paste the final list into the Random Name Picker. The result is selected with a cryptographically secure random source before the wheel animation starts — the spin you see is just the reveal. That's what makes it a genuine draw rather than theater.

Step 4: Make the draw verifiable

Trust comes from witnesses, not claims. Do at least one of these:

  • Screen-record the spin. A 30-second video of the list, the spin, and the result is worth more than any paragraph of explanation.
  • Draw it live. Share your screen on Instagram Live, TikTok Live, or a video call and spin in front of your audience.
  • Publish the entry list (handles only, not emails) so people can confirm they were included.

Step 5: Handle the "no repeat" question

If you're drawing multiple prizes, turn on No repeat picks so the same person can't win twice. Announce the draw order too — "first spin wins the grand prize, next two spins win runner-up prizes" — before you start spinning.

Step 6: Announce and document

Post the winner's name, the date of the draw, and (ideally) the recording. Keep your entry list for a few weeks in case of disputes, then delete it — holding onto people's data longer than needed is bad practice.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Drawing from a messy list. Duplicates and test entries ("asdf", your own name from testing) must be removed first.
  • Changing rules mid-draw. If you said one entry per person, enforce it.
  • Using "pick my favorite comment." That's a judged contest, not a random draw — label it honestly or pick randomly.
  • Forgetting local laws. Giveaway rules vary by country and state, and big prizes can trigger tax or permit requirements. This guide covers the mechanics of a fair draw, not legal advice — check your local regulations for anything beyond a casual giveaway.

After the draw: contacting the winner

Give the winner a clear way to claim the prize — and a deadline. A common pattern: announce publicly, then DM or email the winner with a 48-hour claim window. If they don't respond, spin again for a new winner (with No repeat picks still on, so the original winner can't be redrawn). State the claim window in your rules before the draw so a redraw never looks suspicious.

For physical prizes, confirm the shipping address privately — never post it. For digital prizes (gift cards, codes), send them through a private channel, not in a public comment.

Running giveaways regularly?

If you run monthly draws, save your workflow: keep a template spreadsheet for entries, a rules template you reuse, and a checklist (like the one above). Regular, visibly-fair draws build trust over time — followers start entering because they know your process is clean, which grows each subsequent giveaway.

Quick checklist before you spin

  • ☐ Rules published (who can enter, deadline, draw method)
  • ☐ Entries consolidated into one numbered list
  • ☐ Duplicates, bots, and test entries removed
  • ☐ Prize order decided (grand prize first, then runners-up)
  • ☐ No-repeat mode on if drawing multiple winners
  • ☐ Screen recording or live stream ready

Why a wheel beats a random-number app

A random number generator gives you "winner: #87" — correct, but anticlimactic and hard to verify on video. A wheel shows the whole list, the spin, and the landing in one continuous shot. Your audience watches the result happen instead of taking your word for it.
