Beach Day Pack Wheel: What Goes in the Bag

Last July my family spent forty minutes packing for the beach and still forgot the sunscreen. Now the bag gets packed by wheel: sunscreen, towels, shade, snacks, water, and one book, all picked before anyone leaves the house.

Last July my family spent forty minutes packing for the beach and still forgot the sunscreen. We had three towels for four people, a bag of chips with no drinks, and a beach umbrella with a broken hinge that nobody had checked since the previous summer. My daughter asked where the sunscreen was when we were already on the sand, and I said it was in the bag, which is what you say when you have no idea where the sunscreen is. It was on the kitchen counter, next to the keys, exactly where someone had set it down during the packing argument.

Here’s the thing. Packing a beach bag feels simple until four people pack it together. One person packs snacks. Another person packs towels. Nobody packs water, because everyone assumes someone else packed water. The bag ends up with four versions of the same thing and none of the thing you actually need. So now we pack with the Decision Wheel. The wheel holds the bag categories, and each spin settles one category before anyone zips anything shut. Sunscreen, towels, shade, snacks, water, and one book. Six spins, one bag, and the sunscreen makes it in.

Spin for the sunscreen

Sunscreen comes first because it is the one item that ruins the day when it stays home. A forgotten towel is annoying. A forgotten bottle of sunscreen means someone burns by noon and spends the afternoon under a shirt, annoyed at everyone. Our wheel holds six: spray, lotion, face stick, kids’ formula, aloe, and the big family bottle. The wheel landed on the big family bottle, and the big bottle was the right call for four people, because one bottle covers everyone and nobody has to keep track of four small tubes rolling around in the sand.

I’ll be honest, I used to buy the spray because it felt fast. Spray feels fast and then half of it blows away in the beach wind, and the kids complain about the cold mist on their shoulders. Lotion takes a minute longer and actually stays where you put it. The spin settled it, and settled is better than fast when the whole family shares one bottle.

One rule keeps this spin honest: only put sunscreen on the wheel that is actually in the house. A wheel full of bottles you meant to buy gives you a shopping list, not a packed bag. Check the bathroom cabinet first, put the real bottles on the wheel, and spin. If the wheel lands on a bottle that is nearly empty, that bottle still counts, but it goes on the shopping list for the next trip, in plain sight on the fridge.

Spin for the towels

Towels come second because towel math is where families quietly go wrong. One towel per person sounds right until someone uses a towel as a blanket, another person uses one as a pillow, and the fourth person is drying off with a beach cover-up. Our wheel holds six: one each, one each plus a spare, two big beach towels to share, the old towels, the new striped set, and towels plus a blanket. The wheel landed on one each plus a spare, and the spare saved the day when my son dropped his towel in a tide pool within ten minutes of arriving.

The spare towel also works as a table. Spread it on the sand, put the snacks on it, and the food stays out of the grit. A towel that does two jobs earns its space in the bag, and a bag with a spare towel is a bag that can handle one accident without a trip back to the car.

Count the towels before the bag leaves the house. Not after. After is when you are standing on the sand doing the math out loud, and the math always comes out one short.

Spin for the shade

Shade comes third because the sun at noon is a different sun than the sun at nine in the morning. At nine, the beach feels perfect. At noon, the sand burns your feet and the kids ask to go home. Our wheel holds six: umbrella, tent, tree, hat, canopy, and the car. The wheel landed on umbrella, and the umbrella was the right call for a small group, because it sets up in two minutes and moves when the sun moves.

Check the umbrella before you pack it. Open it in the yard, look at the hinge, and shake it once. An umbrella that breaks on the beach is just a pole and a sad piece of fabric, and you will carry it back to the car while everyone else sits in the sun. I know this because I have carried it. Twice.

If the wheel lands on tree, pick the tree before you leave. A tree sounds free and easy until you arrive and every good spot under a tree is already taken by someone who got there at eight. The wheel picks the shade plan. You still have to get there early enough to use it.

Spin for the snacks

Snacks come fourth because hungry kids at the beach are a countdown, not a mood. The drive home from the beach with hungry kids is the longest drive of the summer. Our wheel holds six: chips, fruit, sandwich, cookies, nuts, and crackers. The wheel landed on chips, and chips were the right call for the sand, because they come in a bag that seals, they do not melt, and they do not care if they sit in the sun for an hour.

Pack the snacks in a bag that closes. An open bowl on the beach collects sand within minutes, and sandy chips are chips nobody eats. A zip bag costs nothing and saves the whole snack. Same rule for fruit: grapes and apple slices travel well, and a banana turns to mush in a hot bag by eleven.

One more rule from a trip I got wrong: pack the snacks last, on top. Snacks buried under towels stay buried, and someone will open the chips in the car on the way home instead of on the beach, which defeats the whole point.

Spin for the water

Water comes fifth because water is the item everyone assumes someone else packed. Chips without water is a dry mouth and a headache by two in the afternoon. Our wheel holds six: water, juice, lemonade, soda, iced tea, and milk. The wheel landed on water, and water was the right call, because juice boxes leak in a hot bag and soda goes flat in the heat.

Freeze half the bottles the night before. Frozen bottles act as ice packs for the snacks on the way there, and they melt into cold water by the time you need them. Two frozen bottles and two cold ones cover a family of four for an afternoon, and the bag stays lighter on the walk back because the water is gone.

Count the bottles out loud. One per person, plus one extra for the drive home. The drive-home bottle is the one people forget, and it is the one they want most.

Spin for the book

The book comes sixth because a beach day has a quiet stretch, and the quiet stretch needs something to fill it. The kids play in the water, the adults sit under the umbrella, and after an hour someone wants a page to read. Our wheel holds six: mystery, romance, memoir, history, poetry, and the book already half-finished on the nightstand. The wheel landed on the half-finished book, and the half-finished book was the right call, because a new book is a gamble and a half-finished book is a story you already care about.

A paperback beats a tablet at the beach. Sand gets into everything, and a paperback with sand in it still reads fine. A tablet with sand in the charging port is a repair bill. If the wheel lands on a hardcover, leave it home and spin again for a paperback, because a hardcover in a beach bag is a brick that gets wet.

One book per adult is enough. Two books per adult is a library that nobody carries. The bag has a weight limit, and the limit is whatever one person can carry from the car to the sand without stopping twice.

Spin for the bag check

The bag check is the last spin, and it is the spin that catches the forgotten thing. Every beach trip has one. The phone charger, the car fob, the dry pouch for the phone, the trash bag for the wrappers. Our wheel holds six: phone, wallet, car fob, dry pouch, trash bag, and keys. The wheel landed on the dry pouch, and the dry pouch saved my phone when a wave came in further than anyone expected.

Run the bag check at the door, not at the car. At the door you can still go back inside. At the car you will say it is probably fine, and it will not be fine. Open the bag, touch each item, and say it out loud. Sunscreen, towels, shade, snacks, water, book, pouch. Seven words, ten seconds, and the trip starts without a U-turn.

If you pack with kids, let the Random Name Picker choose who carries the bag from the car. Carrying the bag is a small job, and a small job picked by a spin feels fair in a way that a parent assigning it never does. For a weekly beach rotation, the Chore Wheel can split the packing jobs across the summer, so the same person is not packing the bag every single Saturday.

Try the beach day pack wheel

Packing a beach bag is six small decisions, and six small decisions are exactly what a wheel is for. Put your real options on the Decision Wheel, spin once per category, and pack what the wheel picks. The bag will not be perfect. It will be packed, and it will have sunscreen in it, which puts it ahead of most beach bags I have ever carried.
