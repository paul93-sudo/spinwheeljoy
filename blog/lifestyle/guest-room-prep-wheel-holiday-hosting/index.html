Guest Room Prep Wheel: Divide Holiday Hosting Chores

Guests arrive Friday and the spare room still holds last summer's boxes. A guest room prep wheel splits the hosting chores so one person is not doing all of it the night before.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published November 1, 2026 Updated November 1, 2026

Your sister texts on Monday. She and her husband arrive Friday at 4, and they stay three nights. You say great, you can’t wait, and then you walk past the spare room and remember what is actually in there. A box of winter coats on the bed. A laundry basket on the chair. The air mattress still in its bag from last Christmas, under a pile of board games nobody plays.

I’ll be honest. Most of the panic before guests arrive is not about the cooking. It is about that room. One person usually ends up doing all of it on Thursday night, tired and a bit resentful, while everyone else watches a show in the living room. A guest room prep wheel fixes that by splitting the work early, while there is still a whole week to do it.

Load the Chore Wheel with the names of everyone in the house. Spin for each job, one job at a time, and write the results on a list stuck to the fridge. The wheel does not make the work disappear. It makes it visible, and visible work gets shared.

Spin for the room first, not the whole house

The mistake is trying to clean the whole house at once. The guest room is the one room that has to be ready by Friday at 4. The garage can wait. The hallway closet can wait. Start with the room your guests will sleep in.

Put the rooms on the wheel: guest room, guest bathroom, hallway, kitchen, living room. Spin once. The guest room will land sooner or later, but spinning for order does something useful. It forces the house to agree on what comes first, and it gives each room a named person.

Here’s the thing. If the guest room lands first, the person who spins it owns that room until it is done. Not “helps with” the room. Owns it. They clear the boxes, change the bed, and say when it is ready. Everyone else works on their own rooms. When every room has an owner, nobody stands in the hallway asking what to do next.

One more rule that saves the week. The room owner also owns the deadline. Guest room done by Wednesday night, not Thursday, not Friday at 3. Wednesday gives you a full day to fix what you forgot. Thursday night prep always turns into Friday morning panic, and Friday morning panic is how guests arrive to a room with no towels.

Beds and bedding before anything fancy

Guests forgive a plain room. They do not forgive a bad bed. A lumpy mattress, a flat pillow, and a thin blanket will color the whole visit, and nobody will tell you about it. They will just sleep badly and leave early on Sunday.

Spin for the bed jobs separately. Put them on the wheel: change the sheets, shake out the pillows, find the extra blanket, clear the nightstand. One spin per job, one name per spin. The person who changes the sheets also checks the mattress pad. If the pad is stained or thin, that is a Wednesday problem, not a Friday surprise.

Wash the sheets even if they look clean. Sheets that sat in a closet since spring smell like a closet. Your guests will not say so. They will just notice. Two sets help a lot. One set on the bed, one set in the closet, so a spill on Saturday night is a ten-minute fix instead of a laundry emergency.

Ask about pillows before Friday. Some people sleep with two flat pillows. Some people need one thick one. A quick text on Tuesday costs nothing: “Two pillows or one?” Then put the answer on the fridge list. Sounds silly, works great. Your sister will sleep better, and you will not spend Friday night hunting for a spare pillow in the linen closet at 11 at night.

The bathroom spin nobody wants but everyone notices

The guest bathroom is the room guests judge most and complain about least. They will not tell you the towels were thin. They will just use their own towel from home for three days and feel a bit awkward about it.

Spin for the bathroom jobs early in the week. Put them on the wheel: fresh towels, soap and shampoo, clean the mirror, empty the trash, stock the toilet paper. Five jobs, five spins, five names if the house is big enough. If the house is small, one person can take two jobs, but write both down. Unwritten jobs are the ones that get forgotten.

Hang two towels per guest, not one. One for the shower, one for the sink, and a hand towel by the basin. Fold them where guests can see them. A towel buried in a drawer might as well not exist. Put a small bottle of shampoo and a bar of soap on the counter even if your guests bring their own. The point is that they do not have to ask.

Empty the trash the morning guests arrive, not the night before. A bathroom trash can fills up fast with packaging and tissues, and an empty can on Friday morning stays empty longer than one emptied Thursday night. The person who owns the bathroom spin also owns the Friday morning check. Ten minutes, one walk through, trash out, towels straight, mirror wiped.

Clear a drawer, a shelf, and a hook

Guests arrive with suitcases, and suitcases need somewhere to go. A guest room with no empty drawer means clothes live on the chair for three days. A room with no hook means coats live on the bed. Both are small annoyances that add up.

Spin for the clearing jobs. Put them on the wheel: empty one drawer, clear one shelf, free two hooks, make floor space for the suitcase. These jobs take twenty minutes each and they are the easiest ones to skip, because the room looks fine without them until the suitcases arrive.

The drawer does not need to be big. One empty drawer per guest is plenty. Fold a couple of hangers on the shelf beside it. If the room has no closet, a luggage rack or a cleared chair works. The point is a visible spot that says “your things go here,” so your guests do not spend the visit apologizing for their suitcase being in the way.

While you are clearing, check the outlets. Guests charge phones, tablets, and sometimes a laptop. One free outlet by the bed beats a power strip buried behind the dresser. If the only outlet is behind the bed, move the bed six inches on Wednesday. Moving it on Friday, with the sheets already on, is a much worse job.

Stock the small things guests never ask for

Nobody asks for a phone charger, a glass of water, or a spare key. They just go without, or they wait until it feels polite to ask, which is usually never. Stocking the small things is the difference between a room and a welcome.

Spin for the stocking jobs. Put them on the wheel: water glasses, phone charger, spare key, tissues, a small trash can. One spin each. The person who stocks the charger checks that it fits a modern phone. An old charger with the wrong plug is worse than no charger, because it looks like you tried and missed.

Put a glass and a small bottle of water on the nightstand. Add a tissue box and a pen. If your guests take medicine at night, a glass of water by the bed saves them a trip to the kitchen in the dark. None of this costs much. All of it says you thought about their first night in the room.

The Decision Wheel can settle the small calls that come up here, like whether the spare key goes on the nightstand or hangs by the front door. One spin, one answer, no ten-minute debate on Thursday night when everyone is already tired.

Food, coffee, and the first-morning plan

The first morning is the real test. Guests wake up in a strange house, unsure whether they can open the fridge, make coffee, or wait for someone else to get up. A plan, told to them on Friday night, removes all of that awkwardness.

Spin for the breakfast jobs before the week starts. Put them on the wheel: buy the coffee, buy the milk and bread, set out the mugs, write the morning note. The morning note is a short card on the kitchen counter: “Coffee is in the cupboard, mugs are here, milk is in the fridge, help yourself.” Guests who read that note relax. Guests who do not read it wait in their room until 9, hungry and polite.

Ask about breakfast on Tuesday, not Friday. “Coffee or tea? Anything you can’t eat?” One text, two answers, and the shopping list writes itself. If your sister drinks tea and her husband drinks coffee, buy both. A house that only stocks coffee for a tea drinker has made a small mistake that lasts the whole visit.

Set the table for the first morning on Friday night, after the guests go to bed. Mugs out, spoons out, bread in the bread box. The person who owns the morning spin does this job, and it takes five minutes. Saturday morning then starts with coffee, not with a hunt through cupboards while everyone is still half asleep.

The last walk-through spin, the night before

Every prep plan needs one final check, and the check needs an owner. Otherwise everyone assumes someone else did it, and nobody did.

Spin for the walk-through on Thursday night. The winner walks the guest room and the guest bathroom with a short list: bed made, towels hung, drawer empty, water glass filled, trash empty, phone charger by the bed, door closes properly. Seven checks, ten minutes, one person.

If the walk-through finds a problem, the person who owned that job fixes it Thursday night, not Friday morning. A missing towel found Thursday night is a two-minute fix. A missing towel found Friday at 4, with guests in the driveway, is a scramble.

The Random Name Picker can draw the walk-through name if the house wants it separate from the chore wheel. Either way, write the name down. A walk-through with no name is a walk-through that never happens.

Try the guest room prep wheel

Guests do not need a perfect house. They need a bed that is made, a towel they can find, a drawer for their clothes, and coffee in the morning without having to ask. That is the whole job, and a wheel splits it across the house in a few minutes on Monday night.

Load the Chore Wheel with the names in your house. Spin for the room, the bed, the bathroom, the drawer, the small things, the breakfast, and the last walk-through. Write every result on the fridge list. Set the guest room deadline for Wednesday night. Then enjoy Friday at 4, when your sister walks into a room that is actually ready, and nobody in the house is tired and resentful about it.
