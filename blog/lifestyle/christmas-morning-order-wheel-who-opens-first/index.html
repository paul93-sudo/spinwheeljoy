Christmas Morning Order Wheel: Who Opens First

It is 6:40 on Christmas morning, the stockings are full, the tree has a pile of gifts under it, and two kids both want to open the first one. A Christmas morning order wheel settles stockings or gifts first, who opens first, the age order, the pace, the big gift, the hander-out, and the breakfast break before anyone tears paper.

It is 6:40 on Christmas morning, and the house is already awake. June, who is six, has been awake since 5:30 and has asked four times if it is time yet. Leo, who is nine, sits on the stairs in his pajamas and holds a candy cane he found in his stocking last night when nobody was looking. Maya has coffee in one hand and a phone in the other, because Grandma is on a video call from two states away and wants to watch the first gift. Tom is standing by the tree with a trash bag, because last year the wrapping paper covered the living room rug by 7:15 and nobody could find the remote.

Everyone wants the same thing, and everyone wants it in a different order. June wants the gifts under the tree first, because the big box by the fireplace has her name on it. Leo wants the stockings first, because his stocking has a small flashlight and he wants to test it in the dark hallway. Maya wants a group photo before the paper flies. Tom wants breakfast first, because he knows that two hungry kids plus a pile of gifts ends with a crying fit before 8:00. Grandma, on the phone, wants everyone to slow down so she can see each gift.

Put it on the Random Name Picker instead. One spin for stockings or gifts first. One for who opens the very first gift. One for the age order. One for the pace. One for the big gift. One for who hands out the gifts. One for the breakfast break. Seven spins, and the morning has an order that nobody voted for and nobody can argue with, because the wheel picked it.

Spin for stockings first or gifts first

Start here, because this call decides the first ten minutes of the morning, and the first ten minutes decide the mood for the rest of it. Stockings first means small gifts, candy, and a slow start. Gifts first means the pile under the tree and a fast start. Breakfast first means nobody opens anything until the pancakes are done, which is a hard sell at 6:40.

Load the wheel with stockings, gifts, breakfast, photos, music, and all at once. Stockings means the stockings come down from the mantel first, which is the right pick when the kids are small, because a stocking gives each kid something in hand fast and buys the adults twenty minutes to make coffee. Gifts means the pile under the tree first, which suits a morning with older kids, because older kids care about the wrapped boxes and not about the candy in a sock. Breakfast means food before paper, which is the right pick when the morning started before 6:00 and everyone is hungry already. Photos means the group photo by the tree first, which suits a morning with Grandma on video call, because a photo after the paper flies shows a living room full of trash. Music means carols on before anything opens, which is the right pick when the house wants a slower start and the adults want five minutes to wake up. All at once means no order at all, which suits a small pile and a family that likes chaos, and nobody else.

Spin once, and that is the order for the morning. The wheel lands on stockings, which suits June fine, because her stocking has a small doll, and suits Leo fine, because his stocking has the flashlight. One rule keeps this spin honest: the winning order holds for the whole morning, not until the first kid gets bored with stockings and points at the tree. A morning that starts with stockings and switches to gifts halfway through the stockings is a morning with two arguments instead of one.

Spin for who opens the very first gift

The stockings are done, and now the pile under the tree is waiting, and both kids are pointing at themselves. This is the spin that causes the most trouble, because the first gift sets the tone, and every kid wants to be the one who tears the first paper.

Load the wheel with youngest, oldest, dad, mom, grandma, and guest. Youngest means the youngest kid in the room opens first, which is the right pick when the youngest kid has been awake since 5:30 and has asked four times if it is time yet. Oldest means the oldest kid opens first, which suits a morning where the oldest kid waited patiently and the youngest kid needs one more minute to understand the order. Dad means Tom opens first, which is the right pick when Tom has a gift from work that everyone wants to see. Mom means Maya opens first, which suits a morning where Maya wrapped every gift and has not opened a single one yet. Grandma means Grandma opens first on video call, which is the right pick when Grandma sent half the pile and wants to watch her gifts open. Guest means the guest in the room opens first, which suits a morning with a friend or a cousin sleeping over, because a guest should not sit and watch for an hour before their turn.

Spin once, and that is the first opener. The wheel lands on youngest, which means June opens the first gift, and Leo gets the second gift, and nobody has to hear a debate about who was awake first. One rule helps: the first opener opens one gift, not three. A first opener who opens three gifts in a row is a first opener who started a second argument.

If the name list turns into a debate about who counts as a guest, settle the order with the Random Name Picker first. One draw for the six roles on the wheel, one spin for the first gift, and the room has an opener.

Spin for the age order

The first gift is open, and now the room needs an order for the rest of the pile. Age order sounds simple until someone asks whether it means kids only, or everyone, or kids first and adults after. Settle it with a spin instead of a family meeting.

Load the wheel with youngest first, oldest first, shortest first, tallest first, birthday order, and spin per gift. Youngest first means the youngest kid opens, then the next youngest, then the next, which is the right pick when the kids are small, because small kids lose patience fast and a long wait ends with tears. Oldest first means the oldest kid opens, then the next oldest, which suits a morning where the oldest kid has been patient and the youngest kid is still busy with a stocking toy. Shortest first means the shortest person opens first, which is the right pick when the room wants a laugh, because shortest first usually means the youngest kid anyway and the adults get to joke about it. Tallest first means the tallest person opens first, which suits a morning where Dad wants to open his gift before he starts handing out the rest. Birthday order means the person with the next birthday opens first, which is the right pick when the ages are close and nobody wants to rank the kids. Spin per gift means no fixed order at all, which suits a big pile, because every gift gets its own spin and nobody waits through a long line.

Spin once, and that is the order for the pile. The wheel lands on youngest first, which means June, then Leo, then Maya, then Tom, then Grandma on the phone. One rule helps: the age order covers the kids first, and the adults take their turns after the kids have each opened two gifts. Adults who wait through the whole kid pile without a turn get grumpy, and grumpy adults make worse pancakes.

If the room cannot agree whether age order means kids only or everyone in the room, settle that call with the Decision Wheel before the second gift. One spin for the rule, and the pile has an order instead of a debate about the order.

Spin for one at a time or free-for-all

The order is set, and now the room needs a pace. One at a time means one gift opens, everyone watches, everyone reacts, and then the next name goes. Free-for-all means everyone tears paper at once, and the morning is over in twelve minutes, and nobody saw what anyone got.

Load the wheel with one at a time, free-for-all, pairs, kids first, adults first, and timer. One at a time means one gift per turn with the whole room watching, which is the right pick when Grandma is on video call, because Grandma cannot watch six gifts open at once on a phone screen. Free-for-all means everyone opens at once, which suits a small pile and a family that likes noise, because a free-for-all with four gifts is fun and a free-for-all with forty gifts is a mess. Pairs means two people open at the same time, which is the right pick when the room has two kids and the adults want both kids to have a gift in hand. Kids first means all the kid gifts open before any adult gift, which suits a morning where the adults wrapped everything and want to watch the kids first. Adults first means the adults open first, which is the right pick when the adults have one gift each and want to get it done before the kid pile starts. Timer means each turn gets sixty seconds, which suits a morning with a big pile and a breakfast reservation, because a timer keeps the pile moving and the morning on schedule.

Spin once, and that is the pace for the morning. The wheel lands on one at a time, which means June opens, everyone watches, Leo opens, everyone watches, and Grandma sees every gift on the phone. One rule helps: the watcher rule. Nobody opens the next gift until the last gift has been shown to the room and to Grandma. A room that skips the showing ends with a pile of paper and a phone call later asking what Leo got.

Spin for when the big gift comes out

Every pile has a big box, and the big box is the gift everyone has been staring at since the tree went up. The question is when it opens. Early means the morning peaks at 7:00 and the rest feels flat. Late means the morning has an ending worth waiting for.

Load the wheel with big last, big first, hide it, after lunch, Santa bag, and surprise. Big last means the big box stays wrapped until the pile is done, which is the right pick when the morning needs an ending, because a big box at the end gives everyone a reason to keep going through the socks and the books. Big first means the big box opens first, which suits a morning where the big gift needs assembly, because a bike or a play kitchen takes an hour and the morning is better with the assembly done early. Hide it means the big box stays hidden until someone finds it, which is the right pick when the big gift is too big for the tree, because a hidden big gift turns the morning into a hunt. After lunch means the big box waits until lunch is over, which suits a family that wants two celebrations in one day, because a morning pile plus an afternoon big gift feels like two Christmases. Santa bag means the big gift comes out of a Santa bag at a set time, which is the right pick when the big gift came from Santa and not from a name on a tag. Surprise means nobody knows when the big box opens, which suits a family that likes suspense, because a surprise big gift keeps the kids guessing through the whole pile.

Spin once, and that is the big gift rule. The wheel lands on big last, which means the big box by the fireplace stays wrapped while the pile goes down gift by gift, and June gets her big box after the last small gift. One rule helps: nobody shakes the big box after the spin. A big box that gets shaken ten times before it opens is a big box with a broken surprise.

If the big gift came from the family name draw, the Secret Santa draw already picked the giver, so the tag reading stays honest and the giver gets their moment when the big box opens.

Spin for who hands out the gifts

The order is set, the pace is set, and now someone has to hand out the gifts. The hander-out reads the tag, carries the gift across the room, and keeps the pile moving. Without a hander-out, the pile turns into a free-for-all, because someone always reaches for the next gift before the last gift has been shown.

Load the wheel with Maya, Tom, Leo, June, Grandma, and rotate. Maya means Maya hands out the gifts, which is the right pick when Maya wrapped every gift and knows which box is which without reading the tag. Tom means Tom hands out the gifts, which suits a morning where Tom is already standing by the tree with the trash bag. Leo means Leo hands out the gifts, which is the right pick when Leo wants a job and not just a turn, because a kid with a job waits better than a kid without one. June means June hands out the gifts, which suits a morning where June wants to carry every box, because a six-year-old with a job feels like part of the morning and not just a watcher. Grandma means Grandma calls out the names from the video call, which is the right pick when Grandma wants a job too and the phone is already on. Rotate means the job changes every five gifts, which suits a long morning, because a hander-out who hands out forty gifts gets tired and starts skipping tags.

Spin once, and that is the hander-out. The wheel lands on Tom, which means Tom reads each tag out loud, carries the gift to the right person, and opens his own gifts after the kids have each opened two. One rule helps: the hander-out reads the tag before the gift moves. A gift handed to the wrong person and opened by the wrong person is a morning story that nobody laughs about until February.

Spin for the breakfast break

The pile is half done, the paper is piling up, and the stomachs are starting to complain. The breakfast break spin decides when the morning pauses for food, because a break too early means cold pancakes, and a break too late means hungry kids and a short fuse.

Load the wheel with before gifts, after stockings, halfway, no break, pancakes, and coffee first. Before gifts means breakfast comes before any gift opens, which is the right pick when the morning started before 6:00 and nobody ate dinner properly last night. After stockings means breakfast comes after the stockings and before the pile, which suits a morning where the stockings bought the adults twenty minutes to cook. Halfway means breakfast comes after half the pile, which is the right pick when the pile is big, because a break in the middle splits the morning into two good halves. No break means snacks only until the pile is done, which suits a small pile, because a small pile takes thirty minutes and breakfast can wait. Pancakes means the break is pancakes no matter what, which is the right pick when pancakes are the family tradition and the kids asked for them last night. Coffee first means the adults get coffee before anything else, which suits a morning where the adults were up late wrapping gifts and the kids were up early shaking boxes.

Spin once, and that is the break. The wheel lands on halfway, which means the pile goes down to the middle, then pancakes, then the rest of the pile, then the big box. One rule helps: the break has a clock on it. Thirty minutes for pancakes, then back to the tree. A break with no clock turns into a morning where the pile sits until noon and the big box opens during lunch.

Try the Christmas morning order wheel

Tonight the tree has a pile, the stockings are full, and the kids are already asking what time the morning starts. The wheel picks stockings first, June first, youngest first, one at a time, big last, Tom handing out, and pancakes halfway through. The family still opens every gift by hand, and the morning still feels like Christmas, because it is. The wheel just stops the part where five people debate the order while the coffee gets cold.

Try it on the Random Name Picker before bed on Christmas Eve. Load the six roles, spin for the first gift, and write the order on a sticky note next to the tree. Sounds silly, works great. The stockings come down, the gifts go around, the pancakes land on time, and the big box opens last, which is more than most Christmas mornings manage.
