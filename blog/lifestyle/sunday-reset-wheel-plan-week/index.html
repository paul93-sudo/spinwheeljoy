Sunday Reset Wheel: Plan the Week in 15 Minutes

Sunday at 8pm used to mean dread. Now it means fifteen minutes, a mug of tea, and a wheel that plans my whole week: one goal, seven dinners, the chores, and one fun thing.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published October 20, 2026 Updated October 20, 2026

On this page

  1. Spin one: the week’s headline goal
  2. Dinners, all seven, in about two minutes
  3. The chore draft nobody can argue with
  4. Workouts that actually happen
  5. The fun one goes first on the calendar
  6. The fifteen-minute timer rule
  7. My two house rules for making it stick

Sunday, 8pm. The weekend is basically a memory and Monday is already leaning on the door. For years my version of “planning the week” was lying on the couch thinking about planning the week, which is not planning. Then Wednesday would arrive with nothing defrosted, a sink full of dishes, and that tight feeling that the week was happening to me.

Now I spend fifteen minutes with a mug of tea and a Decision Wheel. One spin sets the week’s headline goal. A few more spins and I have seven dinners, the chore split, and something fun penciled in for Saturday. Fifteen minutes, and the dread goes quiet.

Spin one: the week’s headline goal

Most of my weeks used to fail for one reason. I never picked what the week was about. So I’d do a little of everything and finish nothing, and by Friday everything felt half done.

Now the first spin of the ritual answers one question: what is the single thing I want done by Friday? I load the wheel with the real candidates from my life. “Clear the garage.” “Finish the report draft.” “Call the dentist, finally.” “Groceries plus meal prep.” Only things I can actually picture being finished.

Whatever it lands on goes at the top of the planner page in big letters. Not a to-do list. One headline. Everything else that week is decoration around it.

I’ll be honest, the first time I did this the wheel picked “clear the garage” and I sat there annoyed for a good ten seconds. Then I did it, because the deal is the deal. The garage is clear. The ten seconds of annoyance was the only part that felt bad.

Dinners, all seven, in about two minutes

The 5pm “what’s for dinner” crisis is the fastest way to lose forty minutes a day. So the second wheel holds my real weeknight dinners. Not aspirational ones. The ones I actually cook: taco night, pasta, stir-fry, sheet-pan chicken, breakfast-for-dinner, soup, leftovers.

I spin seven times, one for each night. If a dinner lands twice in a row, I spin that one again, because nobody wants tacos two nights straight. The full week goes into the planner, and the grocery list writes itself from it.

This pairs well with the what to eat wheel on days when the plan falls apart. Plans are allowed to break. The point of the Sunday plan is that breaking it once doesn’t wreck the whole week, because six other dinners are still standing.

The chore draft nobody can argue with

In my house, “who does the bathroom” was a recurring low-grade war. Not a real fight, just that steady drip of scorekeeping that makes a marriage tired. The wheel ended it in a way no amount of negotiating ever did.

I made a chore wheel with the weekly stuff: vacuum, dishes, trash, bathroom, laundry, grocery run. Whoever spins first gets the first pick of their chore, then the other person spins. Draft-style. It takes ninety seconds.

Here’s the thing: a drafted chore list has a weird psychological effect. Nobody picked the bad chores, so nobody resents the bad chores. They’re just assignments. The bathroom gets cleaned on Tuesdays because the wheel said so, and the wheel doesn’t play favorites.

If you’re splitting with a roommate instead of a partner, the chore wheel handles the weekly rotation version of this, where the jobs reshuffle every Sunday so nobody gets permanently stuck with the trash.

Workouts that actually happen

The fitness apps never worked for me because they asked me to commit to a whole month of routines. Too big. I’d quit by Thursday. What works is deciding this week’s workouts only, one Sunday at a time.

My workout wheel has modest entries: a walk, a swim, a bodyweight circuit, a yoga video, a bike ride, and one “do anything for 20 minutes” slot for the chaotic weeks. I spin three times. Those three workouts go in the planner on specific days, next to real appointments, not floating in the land of “sometime.”

Writing “walk - Tuesday 6pm” in the planner matters. A workout without a time slot is a wish. A workout with a time slot is an appointment. I skip fewer appointments than wishes.

The fun one goes first on the calendar

Every planning system I’ve tried gets this backwards. You plan the work, then squeeze the fun into whatever’s left, and nothing is ever left. My Sunday reset plans the fun first, right on the calendar, before the chores and the workouts.

One spin for the weekend: hike, brunch with friends, movie night, museum, farmer’s market, day trip. It lands where it lands, and it goes on Saturday’s box in ink. Planning the fun first does something real to the rest of the week. Monday feels less like a cage when Saturday already has a plan.

Some Sundays the wheel picks something and I think, “eh.” That’s allowed. The spin is a suggestion, not a court order. But nine times out of ten, “eh” turns out to be exactly right, and I never would have chosen it on my own. That’s the whole point. My brain picks the same three things forever. The wheel has wider taste.

The fifteen-minute timer rule

The ritual only works if it stays short. I set a timer for fifteen minutes. When it goes off, the plan is done, finished or not. This sounds like a gimmick, but it’s the load-bearing wall of the whole thing.

Here’s why. The reason my old planning sessions failed is that they took forty-five minutes and involved a lot of staring. Staring at a planner feels like work but produces nothing. The timer kills the staring. You have fifteen minutes, so you spin, you decide, you write it down, you move on.

Fifteen minutes on Sunday buys back roughly five hours of weeknight dithering. That is the best trade I make all week.

My two house rules for making it stick

Rule one: the plan lives on paper, not in my head. The spins go into a physical planner or one notes page, and then I close it. If it lives in my head, Wednesday-me will not remember what Sunday-me decided, and Wednesday-me is not to be trusted.

Rule two: one redo per week. If a dinner plan falls through, fine, swap it. If a workout gets rained out, move it. But one redo total. Unlimited redos means there is no plan, just suggestions. One redo means the plan bends without breaking.

That’s it. One wheel for the week’s headline goal, one for dinners, one for chores, one for workouts, one for the fun thing. Fifteen minutes, a timer, a mug of tea. Monday morning arrives and for the first time in my adult life, I’m not meeting it unprepared.

Give it a try this Sunday. Open the Decision Wheel, load up your real dinners and your real chores, and spin your way into next week. And if the wheel picks “clear the garage,” well. You know what to do.
