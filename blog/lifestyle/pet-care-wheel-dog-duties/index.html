Pet Care Wheel: Split Dog Duties Fairly

The dog needs walking at 6:40 in the morning whether anyone feels like it or not. Spin for the jobs instead of arguing about them, and the same person stops doing everything.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published October 31, 2026 Updated October 31, 2026

It’s 6:40 on a Tuesday, rain on the kitchen window, and Biscuit is standing by the back door with her leash in her mouth. By the door. Leash in her mouth. Two adults and one teenager are suddenly very interested in their phones.

Someone has to walk her. Last night, Dan said he would. This morning Dan says he meant tomorrow. Maya says she walked her yesterday, which is true, and also true the day before that, because Maya has walked the dog four mornings in a row while everyone else found a reason to be busy. The dog does not care about any of this. The dog needs to go out.

This is the part of getting a dog nobody puts on the adoption form. The fun parts get shared without thinking: the park, the photos, the belly rubs on the couch. The daily jobs land on whoever feels guilty first, and guilt is not a fair system. It just picks the person with the softest heart and hands them every 6:40am walk until they snap.

Load the jobs into the Chore Wheel and spin for them instead. One spin hands out the week’s dog jobs, everyone can see the result, and the argument about who did what last week ends because the wheel keeps no grudges. It just spins.

The morning walk is the job that breaks first

Every dog household has one job that causes more fights than the rest, and it is almost always the first walk of the day. Evening walks are fine. Someone is already up, the day is winding down, and a walk sounds nice. Morning walks are cold, dark in winter, and they compete with showers, school bags, and work calls.

In our house the pattern went like this. One person walked the dog three mornings running because they were “already up.” Then they stopped being already up. Then nobody walked the dog until the dog made the decision for everyone by having an accident by the door, and then everyone felt bad and argued about whose fault that was. A bad system, start to finish.

Put the morning walk on the wheel as its own entry, separate from the evening walk. They are not the same job. One happens when everyone is asleep and grumpy, and the other happens when everyone is awake and bored. If one person gets both walks in the same week, the spin was not fair, it was just fast. Split them, and spin them separately.

Put every dog job on one wheel

A dog is more than walks, and a wheel with only “walk” on it will not fix anything. Sit down once and list every job the dog actually creates in a normal week. Ours looked like this: morning walk, evening walk, breakfast feed, dinner feed, fresh water, poop scoop in the yard, brushing, teeth, medicine, and one play session in the park or the living room.

That list is longer than most people expect, and that is the point. When the jobs are written down, nobody can pretend the dog only needs walking. The person who “never does anything for the dog” usually feeds her every night and just never counted it as a job. The person who “does everything” usually does three jobs and resents a fourth they never agreed to.

Load the full list into the Chore Wheel and let the household see it. The list itself does half the work. Once everyone agrees on what the jobs are, splitting them stops being a fight about memory and starts being a simple spin.

Spin weekly, not daily

Daily spins sound fair and they are not. A daily spin means someone has to remember to spin, someone has to be home to watch it, and someone will miss the spin and claim they never agreed to the result. By Thursday the system has quietly died and Maya is walking the dog again.

Spin once on Sunday night. One spin per job, or one spin that assigns the whole week if your wheel lets you spin down a list. Write the result on the fridge or in the family chat: “This week: Dan walks mornings, Maya feeds, Leo scoops.” One line per person. That line is the whole system for seven days.

A week is also long enough for the result to feel real. A person who lands on morning walks for a week learns what 6:40am actually feels like, and a person who lands on feeding learns that the dog starts staring at them at 5:15pm sharp. Next Sunday, spin again, and the jobs move on.

Feeding is two jobs, not one

Here is a mistake we made in the first month. We put “feeding” on the wheel as a single entry. The person who landed on it fed the dog twice a day, every day, for a week. Fourteen meals. Meanwhile the person who landed on “fresh water” refilled a bowl twice and felt very pleased with themselves.

Split feeding into breakfast and dinner. They are two separate entries on the wheel, and the same person should not land on both in the same week if you can help it. If your wheel lets you remove a name after it lands once, use that. One feeding job per person per week is plenty.

The same logic applies to medicine. If your dog takes a pill twice a day, that is a job with a clock attached to it, and missing it matters more than missing a brushing session. Put medicine on the wheel as its own entry, and give it to an adult, not to a kid who might forget on a school trip day. Some jobs are fine to hand to anyone. Medicine is not one of them.

Vet trips count double

Not every dog job costs the same amount of time and patience. A walk takes twenty minutes. A vet trip takes half a morning, a car ride with a nervous dog, a waiting room, and a bill. Treating them as equal entries on the wheel is how someone ends up doing the vet trip and the bath in the same week while someone else refills the water bowl and calls it even.

Give the big jobs weight. In our house, a vet trip or a grooming appointment counts as two jobs. The person who does it skips the next spin, or takes only one small job the following week. You can run this with a simple house rule: big job this week means a light week next week. Write it on the fridge next to the spin result so nobody has to remember it.

If two people disagree about whether a job is big, that is a useful argument to have once. Settle it, write it down, and stop having it. Our list says vet, groomer, and bath are big. Everything else is normal. Your list might differ, and that is fine, as long as it is written down before the spin and not invented after someone lands on a job they dislike.

If the household cannot agree on the weighting, spin for it with the Decision Wheel. One spin, one answer, no more debate. The wheel is better at ending these talks than any family meeting I have sat through.

The yard job needs its own rotation

Poop scoop is the job everyone trades away. It is also the job that causes the most creative excuses. “My back hurts.” “It is raining.” “I did it last time,” said by someone who did it three weeks ago.

Scoop duty works best on a faster rotation than everything else. Walks and feeding change weekly. Scoop changes daily, or every other day if the yard is small. A short rotation means nobody stares at a whole week of it on the fridge and starts negotiating on Monday. It also means the job never piles up into a Saturday nightmare that takes forty-five minutes and a hose.

One more rule helps: scoop duty cannot be traded more than once in a week. Trades sound harmless, but they always flow one way, toward the person who feels guilty saying no. If someone is sick or out, respin instead of trading. A respin is public and fair. A quiet trade in the hallway is how one person ends up holding the scoop bag again.

Give kids real jobs, not pretend ones

Kids want a dog job. They do not want a fake one. Handing a child a job that does not matter, like “official dog hugger,” teaches them that their help is decoration. Give them a job the dog actually needs, sized to what they can really do.

An eight-year-old can refill the water bowl, brush the dog for five minutes, and put kibble in the bowl while an adult watches the amount. A twelve-year-old can do the evening walk with an adult nearby, or the full walk alone if the dog is calm and the route is safe. A teenager can take a full morning walk. The 6:40am one counts too, and they should take it sometimes. The dog does not care about age. The dog cares that someone shows up.

Spin for kids’ jobs from a smaller wheel, not the adult wheel. Load only the jobs that fit the child, and let them spin their own. A child who spins their own job argues less about it than a child who is handed one. If you have more than one child, the Random Name Picker settles who goes first when two of them want the same job, which in our house is always the evening walk, because that is the one that ends at the park.

Two rules keep the spin honest

A rotation only works while people trust it, so keep two rules and keep them short.

First, being out means a respin, not a debt. If the wheel lands on someone who is sick, traveling, or working late, spin again. That person does not owe the household a make-up walk, and they do not lose their turn in the rotation. A system that punishes a sick day gets gamed within a month, usually by people suddenly feeling unwell on scoop days.

Second, nobody gets the morning walk two weeks in a row. If the wheel lands on last week’s morning walker, spin again. Back-to-back 6:40am weeks are fine in theory, but the joke about a rigged wheel stops being funny the second time, and the whole system runs on people believing the spin.

Post both rules where the spin result lives. “This week: Dan walks mornings. Rules: out means respin, no back-to-back mornings.” When a new person joins the household routine, a babysitter or a grandparent staying for a month, they can read the system in one line instead of learning it by watching for weeks.

Try it at the next Sunday spin

Open the Chore Wheel, type in your dog’s real jobs, and spin for next week before Sunday dinner ends. Post the result where everyone can see it. That is the entire setup.

Watch the first two weeks. The 6:40am walk will still be cold, and the dog will still stare at the door like the world depends on it. But the argument about whose turn it is will be gone, because the wheel already answered it. And Maya, or whoever your Maya is, will get a morning off, which is the whole point. A dog is supposed to be the best part of a household, not the reason it argues before breakfast.
