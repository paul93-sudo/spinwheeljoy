How to show SpinWheelJoy in a stream

  1. Open your chosen tool in a separate browser window and prepare a non-sensitive test list.
  2. In your streaming or meeting software, choose the browser window as a capture source or use a regular screen-sharing feature.
  3. Adjust the window so viewers can read the names, wheel, and result. Test both landscape and phone-sized layouts if your audience uses mobile.
  4. Enable sound with a real click or tap if you want audio. Confirm that your capture setup includes the correct audio source.
  5. Run one full test round, including the final winner screen and replay behavior, before inviting participants.

What works today and what does not

These tools run in a web browser and can be displayed using standard screen sharing or window capture. This guide does not claim native OBS, YouTube, Twitch, or TikTok integration, automatic chat participant imports, audience QR joining, or remote host controls. Those features require separate implementations and testing.

Keep participant data private

Do not project email addresses, full private names, account identifiers, or data entered without consent. When you need a public on-screen list, use display names or pseudonyms and review it before streaming. Avoid placing sensitive information into shareable URLs.

Audio and performance checklist

On iPhone or iPad Safari, game audio may require a direct tap before it starts. Verify mute and unmute, screen rotation, and playback after returning from another app. On desktop, test the stream's captured audio separately from the browser's speakers; hearing an effect locally does not prove viewers can hear it.

Tip: start with the main Spin the Wheel for a quick audience question. Switch to Duck Dash when you want a longer, animated reveal. At present, copy or re-enter lists as needed between tools; a shared Joy Session is planned, not launched.
