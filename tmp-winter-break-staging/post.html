Winter Break Activity Wheel: Keep the Kids Busy Until School Restarts

Winter break runs about two weeks, and kids run out of ideas by day three. Put the day's activities on a wheel, spin once per block, and let the wheel carry the plan.

By SpinWheelJoy Team Published October 26, 2026 Updated October 26, 2026

On this page

  2. Get outside once before lunch
  3. Spin for the quiet hour after lunch
  4. Let the wheel settle screen time
  5. Spin for the evening game
  6. One spin for the helper job
  7. Spin tomorrow tonight

It was 8:40 on the first Saturday of winter break when my daughter walked into the kitchen and said the words every parent dreads in December: “I’m bored.” School had been out for one day. One. My son was still in pajamas at 8:40, building a fort out of couch cushions, and he had already asked what we were doing three times.

Here’s the thing. Winter break is long. About two weeks long, with cold weather, short days, and kids who are used to a school bell telling them what happens next. Take the bell away and the day has no shape. Kids fill that gap by asking you, over and over, what happens next. By day three, you are out of answers and patience in equal measure.

A wheel gives the day its shape back. Load the activities into the Decision Wheel, spin once for each block of the day, and the plan comes from the wheel instead of from you. Kids argue with parents. They rarely argue with a wheel they watched spin.

Spin the morning first, not the whole day

Do not try to plan the full day in one sitting. A full-day plan falls apart at the first snack request, and then you are back to answering questions anyway. Plan one block at a time.

Start with the morning. After breakfast, spin once. That spin picks the morning activity, and the morning runs until lunch. No second spin because someone groans. The groan lasts about four minutes. I timed it once, out of curiosity, when the wheel landed on crafts and my son announced crafts were boring. He was elbow-deep in glitter glue by 10:15.

Keep the morning list short. Six options is plenty: sled, craft, puzzle, bake, movie, game. A list of twenty options turns the spin into another debate, this time about the list. Six options, one spin, done.

Get outside once before lunch

Cold air does real work on a winter break day. A kid who has been outside for forty minutes comes back in calmer, hungrier, and ready to sit still for a while. A kid who has stayed inside since breakfast will bounce off the walls by noon. You know this. I know this. The hard part is getting everyone out the door.

Spin for it. Put the outside options on the wheel: sled, walk, snowman, scavenger hunt, skate, game. Whatever lands is what you do. The spin removes the negotiation about whether to go out at all, and that negotiation is the part that eats the morning.

On our street, the wheel landed on snowman three days in a row one year. Sounds silly, works great. The first snowman was small and lopsided. The second got a carrot nose and a scarf. The third became a whole snow family, with a snow dog that my daughter insisted on. Three days of outside time, zero arguments about going out, because the wheel said so.

Spin for the quiet hour after lunch

Lunch ends and the house gets loud again. This is the block most parents get wrong, because they try to fill it with another big activity. Kids do not need another big activity after lunch. They need a quiet hour, and they will fight it if you call it a quiet hour.

Call it a spin instead. Put six calm options on the wheel: read, puzzle, draw, Lego, craft, music. Spin once. The winner runs for about an hour, with hot chocolate if you want to sweeten the deal.

The trick is that the wheel makes the quiet hour feel like a choice. My son picked puzzle on a spin last year and spent fifty minutes on a 300-piece puzzle of a lighthouse, in silence, next to his sister who was drawing. Fifty minutes of quiet, in December, with two kids in the same room. I sat on the couch and did nothing, which felt like a small miracle.

Set a timer you can all see. When the timer rings, the quiet hour is over, and nobody has to argue about whether it lasted long enough. The timer decides. That is the same idea as the wheel, just for time.

Let the wheel settle screen time

Screen time is the winter break fight that never really ends. Kids ask for it in the morning. They ask for it after lunch. They ask for it while you are cooking dinner. If you decide case by case, you will decide it twenty times a day, and every decision will feel unfair to someone.

Spin for it once. Put the options on the wheel: movie, game, show, app, music, off. Yes, off belongs on the wheel. If the wheel lands on off, screens stay off for that block, and the next spin picks something else. Kids accept a wheel landing on off far better than they accept a parent saying no for the tenth time.

One rule keeps this fair: the screen spin happens at a set time, not whenever someone asks. In our house it is 3 pm. Before 3 pm, the answer to “can I play a game” is “we spin at 3.” After 3 pm, the wheel answers. That one rule cut the asking down to almost nothing, because asking early never changed the answer.

Spin for the evening game

Evenings in winter break can drift. Dinner ends, everyone scatters, and by 8 pm someone is bored again and someone else is on a tablet. A single evening spin stops the drift.

Put the evening options on the wheel: board, cards, charades, dice, quiz, movie. Spin after dinner, while everyone is still at the table. The winner is the evening plan, and everyone plays, even the person who wanted a different game. That last part matters. If one person opts out every night, the evening game dies within a week.

Keep a small box of games by the table during break, so the spin never lands on a game you cannot find. Charades needs nothing but a list of words on slips of paper. A quiz can be ten questions you make up about the family. Dice games need two dice and a piece of paper. The wheel only works if the winning option is ready to play in five minutes.

One spin for the helper job

Winter break still has chores, and kids still avoid them. A wheel can split the daily jobs without a lecture. Put the jobs on the wheel: set the table, feed the dog, take out the trash, wipe the counter, load the dishwasher, sweep. Spin once per kid, and take each job off the wheel as it lands.

Write the jobs down before the spin, so nobody argues about what counts. Feeding the dog means food and fresh water, both of them. Setting the table means plates, cups, and forks for everyone, all three.

Rotate the wheel each day, and let kids spin for themselves. A kid who spins their own job complains less about it. That is not a theory. I watched my daughter spin “take out the trash,” sigh loudly, and then do it in three minutes flat, because the wheel had spoken and there was nobody left to argue with.

Spin tomorrow tonight

This is the spin that changes the mornings. Before bed, spin for tomorrow morning. Write the result on a sticky note on the fridge: tomorrow morning is sled, or craft, or bake. Then the morning starts with an answer instead of a question.

Kids sleep better when they know what morning holds, and parents wake up better too. No more 8:40 “I’m bored” before you have had coffee. The sticky note answers it. The morning plan is already made, by a wheel, last night, when everyone was in a good mood.

If the weather changes overnight, spin again in the morning. That is the one fair reason for a second spin. Rain instead of snow, or a cold snap that makes sledding miserable, is a real change. A groan is not a real change. Keep that line clear and the wheel keeps working all break long.

Try the winter break activity wheel

Tonight, load six morning activities into the Decision Wheel and spin for tomorrow morning. Write the result on the fridge. Then add the outside spin, the quiet-hour spin, and the evening-game spin, one block at a time, over the next few days.

If the group cannot agree on the activity list itself, the Yes or No Wheel can settle whether a new activity joins the list. And if dinner turns into its own debate, the What to Eat Wheel handles that spin too. One wheel per decision, one spin per block, and winter break gets a shape that holds until school starts again.
