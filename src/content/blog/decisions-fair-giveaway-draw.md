"Was it rigged?" — three words that can sour an otherwise great giveaway. When you pick a winner behind closed doors, even honest draws look suspicious. The fix is embarrassingly simple: **draw in public, with a wheel everyone can watch.**

## The transparent draw method

1. Collect entries as a simple list — usernames, order numbers, or emails, one per line.
2. Open the [Random Name Picker](/tools/random-name-picker/) and paste the list.
3. Screen-record the spin (or do it live).
4. Post the video with the winner announcement.

A visible wheel slowing down to a name is dramatically more convincing than "we used a random generator, trust us." People can *see* fairness happening.

![Hand tapping spin on a phone showing a colorful prize wheel](/assets/blog/inline-phone-spin.svg)

## Rules that build trust

- **Publish the entry list** (or its count) before the draw, so nobody can be added after.
- **One entry per person** — dedupe the list first, and say so.
- **Draw once, on camera.** Re-spins destroy credibility. If the wheel glitches mid-spin, say so openly and redo the whole draw.
- **No-repeat mode for multiple prizes.** Drawing three winners? Enable no-repeat picks so one person cannot sweep all the prizes.

## What NOT to do

Do not use these tools for lotteries, raffles, or anything where money changes hands or regulations apply — they are not certified for regulated drawings. For casual giveaways, promo winners, and classroom prizes, a public wheel draw is the sweet spot: fun to watch, easy to trust.

## After the draw

Announce the winner with the video, tag them if appropriate, and keep the entry list for a week in case of questions. Transparency is a habit — your next giveaway will get more entries because people saw the last one was fair.
